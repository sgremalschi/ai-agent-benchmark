Experiment 015 — Thread-safe in-memory job scheduler

Implement JobScheduler in problems/job_scheduler.py.

Read the existing starter implementation and preserve its public API.

Requirements:
1. Use an injected clock, defaulting to time.monotonic.
2. schedule(job_id, run_at, callback) registers a pending job.
3. Duplicate IDs are rejected with ValueError while a job is pending or executing.
4. IDs may be reused after execution or cancellation.
5. run_due() processes jobs due at the instant it begins.
6. Execute in ascending run_at order, breaking ties by insertion order.
7. Each callback is attempted at most once.
8. Return the number of callbacks attempted, including those that raise exceptions.
9. Callback exceptions must not prevent other jobs from executing.
10. Never invoke callbacks while holding an internal lock.
11. Callbacks may schedule or cancel other jobs.
12. A due job remains cancellable until its callback starts.
13. Jobs scheduled during run_due() must wait for a subsequent invocation.
14. Concurrent run_due() calls must never execute the same job twice.
15. shutdown() is idempotent and rejects future schedule() calls with RuntimeError.
16. Pending jobs remain executable after shutdown().
17. All public operations must be thread-safe.
18. Do not create background threads or use external dependencies.

Implementation constraints:
- Prefer simple, maintainable Python.
- Use standard-library synchronization primitives.
- Avoid busy-waiting.
- Do not modify the caller's data.
- Do not commit or push.

Testing:
- Create tests/test_job_scheduler.py.
- Include at least 15 meaningful tests.
- Cover ordering, cancellation, exceptions, shutdown, reentrancy,
  concurrent scheduling, and concurrent execution.
- Run pytest and Ruff on the files you modify.

Allowed modifications:
- problems/job_scheduler.py
- tests/test_job_scheduler.py

Do not modify other files.
